Testimonies: Aramean & Syriac Synonymy Across History

  1. Research
  2. Sources
  3. Testimonies

This collection of testimonies from ancient scholars, medieval church fathers, and modern historians documents the continuous identity of the Aramean people across three millennia. These voices - both from within the community and from external observers - consistently affirm that "Aramean" and "Syriac" refer to the same people, providing powerful evidence of cultural and identity continuity through self-recognition and historical witness.

Poseidonios of Apamea

135 BC - 51 BC
"The people we Greeks call Syriacs were called by the Syriacs themselves Aramaeans"
J.G. Kidd, Posidonius (Cambridge Classical Texts and Commentaries, 1988), vol. 2, pt. 2, pp. 955-956

Strabo from Amasya

63 BC - 24 AD
"Syriacs are by the Syriacs themselves called Arimaeans and Aramaeans"
The Geography of Strabo, translated by Horace Leonard Jones, Loeb Classical Library (1917), Book I, Ch. 2, 34

Yosef ben Matityahu (Josephus)

37 AD - 100 AD
"Aram had the Arameans, which the Greeks called Syriacs"
Antiquities of the Jews, translated by William Whiston (1737), Book I, Ch. 6

Eusebius Pamphili

c. 275 - 339 AD
"from Aram the Arameans, which are also called Syriacs"
Sebastian Brock, "Eusebius and Syriac Christianity," in Eusebius, Christianity, and Judaism (Leiden 1992), p. 226

Jacob of Edessa

c. 640 - 708 AD
"we the Arameans, that is to say Syriacs"
Maurice Brière, Les Homiliae Cathedrales de Sévère d'Antioche (Patrologia Orientalis 29, 1922), p. 196

Al-Masudi

896 - 956 AD
"Tur Abdin is the mountain where remnants of the Aramean Syriacs still survive."
R. Duval (ed.), Lexicon Syriacum (Paris, 1888-1901), p. 1323-1324

Hassan Bar Bahlul

963 AD
"The Syriacs were formerly called Arameans"
R. Duval (ed.), Lexicon Syriacum (Paris, 1888-1901), p. 1323-1324

Michael the Great

1126 - 1199 AD
"the Arameans who are the Syriacs"
J-B Chabot, Chronique de Michel le Syrien Patriarche Jacobite d'Antioche (Paris, 1899), Livre II, Chapitre I

Dionysius Bar Salibi

1166 - 1171 AD
"We are the Arameans that is Syriac Orthodox"
Medieval Syriac theological writings

Gregorios Bar Hebraeus

1226 - 1286 AD
"You have brought me to the eloquent Aramean-Syriac nation."
The Book of Rays (Ketbo d-Heyutho)

Professor Theodor Mommsen

1817 - 1903
"the history of the Aramaean or Syriac nation"
The History of Rome (1854-1856), translated by William Purdie Dickson, Book First, Chapter One

Professor Theodor Nöldeke

1836 - 1930
"the name of the 'Syriacs' the people who called themselves 'Aramaeans'"
Compendious Syriac Grammar, translated by James A. Crichton (London, 1904), introduction

Professor Rubens Duval

1839 - 1911
"the Arameans who after the evangelisation of these countries adopted the name Syriacs"
Anciennes Littératures Chrétiennes: La Littérature Syriaque (3rd ed., Paris 1907), p. 4

Max von Oppenheim

1860 - 1946
"Today's Syriacs are regarded largely as the descendants of the old Arameans"
Vom Mittelmeer zum persischen Golf durch den Haurän (Berlin 1899), Band I, p. 6

Patriarch Aphrem Barsaum

1887 - 1957
"The Syriac community was known from its beginning as the Aramean community"
"Sefro Suryoyo" [The Syriac Writing] (Aleppo, 1947), p. 52

Patriarch Ignatius Zakka Iwas

1933 - 2014
"Those who want to make a difference between Syriacs and Arameans make a big mistake."
Den syriska kyrkan av Antiokia genom tiderna, p. 10

Continuity Through Recognition

These testimonies from across three millennia - ancient Greek scholars, medieval Syriac church fathers, modern European historians, and contemporary church leaders - all affirm the same fundamental truth: the Aramean and Syriac peoples are one and the same. This identity has been maintained through self-recognition, historical witness, and continuous testimony across religions, empires, and centuries. The evidence is not merely linguistic or administrative, but deeply rooted in the lived identity and self-understanding of the people themselves and those who have documented their history.
