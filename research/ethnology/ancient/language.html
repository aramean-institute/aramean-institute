Aramaic is a Northwest Semitic language with ancient and modern varieties. This page focuses on the ancient stages most relevant to early Aramean culture and society: Old Aramaic, Official Aramaic, and Middle Aramaic.

Language and Cultural Identity in the Ancient Period

Period: c. 1200-900 BC

During the period of Aramean emergence, proto-Aramaic or early Aramaic dialects may have been spoken by some groups later identified as Arameans. Direct evidence for the 12th-11th centuries BC remains limited, so language should be treated as one element of cultural history rather than as proof of a single, unified Aramean identity.

  • Semitic foundation - Aramaic belongs to the Northwest Semitic branch and is closely related to languages such as Phoenician, Hebrew, and Ugaritic.
  • Dialect diversity - Different communities and tribal groups likely used distinctive speech varieties; the evidence becomes clearer through later inscriptions.
  • Oral tradition and writing - Communication was primarily oral. The earliest secure Aramaic inscriptions date to the early first millennium BC, using an alphabetic tradition related to neighboring West Semitic scripts.
  • Language and identity - Aramaic could contribute to shared cultural affiliation, but language alone cannot establish continuous identity for all earlier groups.
