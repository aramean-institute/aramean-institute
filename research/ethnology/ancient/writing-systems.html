Scripts and Orthography of Aramaic

Aramaic has been written in multiple script systems across its long history, reflecting different periods, regional traditions, and religious communities. Understanding Aramaic writing systems provides insight into linguistic standardization, cultural identity, and the transmission of religious and literary texts.

Historical Script Development

Old Aramaic Script (1100-700 BC)

Early script forms developed from Phoenician writing systems:

  • Angular, monumental forms
  • Alphabetic writing system (22 consonants)
  • Used for royal inscriptions and monumental texts
  • Examples: Tel Fakhariyah inscription, Hadad inscription

Official Aramaic Script (700-300 BC)

Standardized script for imperial administration:

  • Formal, regular letter forms
  • Systematic orthographic conventions
  • Used in papyri, ostraca, and administrative documents
  • Basis for later script developments

Square Script Development (4th century BC onward)

The transition to square script forms occurred during the Hellenistic period:

  • Geometric, angular letterforms
  • Influenced by Greek and other writing traditions
  • Became standard for Jewish Aramaic texts
  • Basis for Hebrew square script

Alphabetic Systems and Orthography

Consonantal vs. Vowel Notation

Ancient script traditions generally represented consonants explicitly, while vowel values were inferred from context or marked through limited conventions such as matres lectionis.
