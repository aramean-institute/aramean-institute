Aramaic stands as one of the world's most historically significant languages, with a continuous tradition spanning over three millennia. The Aramean language has evolved through multiple historical phases, from ancient inscriptions to modern dialects.


Aramaic linguistic history includes related Syriac traditions, modern varieties, writing systems, and ongoing research into the language's development and regional variation.

Aramaic is a Northwest Semitic language with ancient and modern varieties. A five-phase periodization associated with the linguistic scholarship of Joseph Augustine Fitzmyer and Aaron Butts identifies: Old Aramaic, Official Aramaic, Middle Aramaic, Late Aramaic, and Modern Aramaic.

These phases provide a chronological framework for organizing the surviving linguistic evidence. Their dates and boundaries are approximate, and regional varieties could overlap; the sequence should not be understood as a uniform development experienced by every Aramaic-speaking community.
