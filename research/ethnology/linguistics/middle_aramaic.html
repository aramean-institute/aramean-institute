Middle Aramaic refers to the transitional phase of the Aramaic language (c. 300 BC - 200 AD) between the Imperial (Official) Aramaic period and the later literary dialects. During this period, Aramaic diverged into distinct regional varieties as the political unity of the Persian Empire gave way to Hellenistic and Roman rule. The east-west dialectal split that would define later Aramaic became increasingly pronounced.

Biblical Aramaic

Timeframe: c. 450 BC - 165 BC
Spoken in: Judea and the Jewish diaspora (Babylon, Levant)
Key Inscription: Books of Daniel and Ezra (Hebrew Bible)
Alphabet: א ב ג ד ה ו ז ח ט י כ ל מ נ ס ע פ צ ק ר ש ת

The form of Aramaic preserved in the biblical books of Daniel and Ezra. It represents a literary register closely related to Imperial Aramaic, used for narrative and administrative passages within the Hebrew Bible. The Ezra passages likely date from the 5th century BC and the Daniel passages from the 2nd century BC.

Nabatean

Timeframe: c. 200 BC - 328 AD
Spoken in: Nabataean Kingdom (modern Jordan, Southern Levant, and Northern Arabia)
Key Inscription: Inscriptions of Petra and Hegra
Alphabet: 𐢀 𐢁 𐢂 𐢃 𐢄 𐢅 𐢆 𐢇 𐢈 𐢉 𐢊 𐢋 𐢌 𐢍 𐢎 𐢏 𐢐 𐢑 𐢒 𐢓 𐢔 𐢕

Spoken and written in the Nabataean Kingdom for administration and trade, this variant heavily influenced the later development of the Arabic script. According to historical Syriac sources, the Nabataeans are explicitly classified as Arameans, sharing a direct ethnic and linguistic lineage within the broader Aramean heritage.

Jewish Old Palestinian

Timeframe: c. 200 BC - 200 AD
Spoken in: Judea, Galilee, and the broader Levant
Key Inscription: Dead Sea Scrolls, early Targumim
Alphabet: א ב ג ד ה ו ז ח ט י כ ל מ נ ס ע פ צ ק ר ש ת

A Western Aramaic dialect spoken by Jews in Hasmonean, Herodian, and Roman Judea. It is the language of many Dead Sea Scroll texts and the earliest Palestinian Targumim. This is the everyday Aramaic of the Second Temple period, including the time of Jesus. It later evolved into the Jewish Palestinian Aramaic of the Talmudic period.

Hatran

Timeframe: c. 100 BC - 241 AD
Spoken in: Hatra (modern-day Iraq) and Eastern Mesopotamia
Key Inscription: Hatran Inscriptions
Alphabet: 𐣠 𐣡 𐣢 𐣣 𐣤 𐣥 𐣦 𐣧 𐣨 𐣩 𐣪 𐣫 𐣬 𐣭 𐣮 𐣯 𐣰 𐣱 𐣲 𐣪 𐣴 𐣵

This dialect was specific to the semi-autonomous trading city of Hatra. It retained strong architectural and religious textual presence until the fall of the city to the Sasanian Empire.

Palmyrean

Timeframe: c. 44 BC - 274 AD
Spoken in: Palmyra (Central Syrian Desert)
Key Inscription: Palmyrene Tariff and funerary bust inscriptions
Alphabet: 𐡠 𐡡 𐡢 𐡣 𐡤 𐡥 𐡦 𐡧 𐡨 𐡩 𐡪 𐡫 𐡬 𐡭 𐡮 𐡯 𐡰 𐡱 𐡲 𐡳 𐡴 𐡵

A distinct West Aramaic dialect used in the wealthy caravan city of Palmyra, utilizing a highly stylized, cursive variant of the Aramaic alphabet.
