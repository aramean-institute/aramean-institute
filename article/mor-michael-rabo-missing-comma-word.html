A missing word and comma in a copied manuscript of the Chronicle of Michael the Great altered the meaning of a key passage on Aramean identity.

The Chronicle of Michael the Great (Mor Michael Rabo), the largest surviving Syriac historical work, is preserved primarily through a single manuscript known as the Edessan-Aleppo codex. Written by the Syriac Orthodox Patriarch of Antioch (1166-1199), the Chronicle covers world history from creation to Michael's own time and remains one of the most important sources for the history of the Syriac-speaking peoples. During the copying process, this manuscript suffered textual corruption that would have significant consequences for modern scholarship on Aramean identity.

The Error

In his edition of the Chronicle [1], published between 1899 and 1910, French orientalist Jean-Baptiste Chabot reproduced the corrupted text without correcting the known scribal errors. Despite the heading listing the sons of Shem, the word "Oromoye" (Arameans) was missing from the passage. However, on page 11 of the Chronicle, the correct reading is confirmed: "Bnay Shim: Othuroye, Kaldoye, Ludoye, Oromoye, d-henun Suryoye, Ebroye, Forsoye" - meaning "Children of Shem: Assyrians, Chaldeans, Lydians, Arameans, that is Syriacs, Hebrews, and Persians." In the corrupted copy, the word "Oromoye" was dropped and the comma was lost, producing the reading "Othuroye d-henun Suryoye" - "Assyrians that is Syriacs."

Significance

This scribal error fundamentally altered the meaning of the text. Where Michael the Great had identified the Arameans as synonymous with Syriacs - a well-attested equivalence in Syriac literature - the corrupted version falsely attributed this identity to the Assyrians instead. The error effectively erased the Arameans from the genealogical list of Shem's descendants.


The equivalence between "Aramean" and "Syriac" is not unique to Michael's Chronicle. It is consistently affirmed throughout Syriac literary tradition. The term "Suryoyo" (Syriac) was understood by Syriac authors as a Greek-derived synonym for "Oromoyo" (Aramean), referring to the same people and language. Michael the Great himself, elsewhere in the Chronicle, explicitly identifies his people as Arameans who are called Syriacs by the Greeks.

Ongoing Impact

The uncorrected reading continues to circulate online, often cited without awareness of the scribal corruption, generating considerable confusion regarding Aramean ethnic identity, particularly among diaspora communities where the Chronicle is regarded as an authoritative historical source.


Because Chabot's edition was for decades the only widely accessible version of the Chronicle, the corrupted reading became embedded in secondary literature and online discussions. Many who encounter the text assume they are reading Michael the Great's own words, unaware that the passage they cite is the product of a copyist's error rather than the patriarch's original composition.

Correction

The error was rectified in a 2006 critical edition of the Chronicle (Ktābā d-Maktbānút zabne) [2], prepared through the collaborative efforts of Fr. Dayroyo Isa Aygür, Mushe Görgün, Steffanos Budak, Iskender Demirel, Eliyo Seven Beth Qemez, and Gabriel Yalgin, who restored the original reading based on textual analysis and comparison with other sources.


The restored text confirms what the broader context of the Chronicle already makes clear: Michael the Great listed the Arameans among the sons of Shem and identified them - not the Assyrians - as those who are called Syriacs.
