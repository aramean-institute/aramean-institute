According to ancient Syriac tradition, Jesus fulfilled His promise to King Abgar V Ukama by sending the Apostle Addai to Bet Urhay to proclaim the Gospel to the Arameans and bring healing to the king.


After the ascension of Jesus Christ, the apostles began spreading the Gospel throughout many lands. According to the Syriac Christian tradition preserved in the Doctrine of Addai, the Apostle Thomas appointed Addai (Thaddeus) to travel to Aram, the land of the Arameans in Syria and Mesopotamia.[1]


Addai journeyed to Urhay (Edessa), the capital of the Kingdom of Osroene, where King Abgar V Ukama was said to have suffered from a severe illness. According to tradition, Abgar had previously written to Jesus seeking healing, and Jesus replied with a promise to send one of His disciples after His earthly ministry had been completed.[2]


When Addai arrived in Urhay, he preached about the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Many Arameans gathered to hear his message and learn about the faith that had begun in Judea.[3]


Through the power of God, Addai healed the sick, cast out evil spirits, and performed signs that amazed the people. His preaching and miracles drew large crowds and helped spread the Christian message throughout the city and surrounding region.


Addai presented the Gospel to King Abgar and the people of Urhay. According to the Doctrine of Addai, the king received his teaching with faith and witnessed the miracles performed by the apostle.[4]


Addai then prayed over the king, and Abgar was healed from his illness.[4]


The conversion of the king greatly influenced the people of the kingdom, and many accepted the Christian faith. According to Syriac Christian tradition, the Arameans of Bet Urhay became the first people whose ruler and kingdom accepted Christianity.[5]


Addai baptized new believers and established one of the earliest Christian communities in the region. The Church of Urhay later became an important center of Syriac Christianity, preserving and developing the Syriac language, a literary form of Aramaic closely related to the language spoken by Jesus and His disciples.[6]


Addai continued preaching throughout Syria and neighboring regions, teaching repentance, faith, and devotion to God. His mission helped spread Christianity among the Arameans and laid the foundation for the growth of the Syriac Church for centuries to come.[7]


Ancient Christian writings remember Addai as one of the first apostles to bring the Gospel to the peoples of Mesopotamia and Syria, and his mission remains a central part of the Syriac Christian tradition.[8]
