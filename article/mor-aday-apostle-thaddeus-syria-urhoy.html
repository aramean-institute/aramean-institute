Jesus sent the Apostle Thaddeus (Addai) to the Arameans of Syria to proclaim the Gospel and to heal King Abgar Ukama of Edessa.

⏱ -

After the ascension of Jesus Christ, the apostles began spreading the Gospel throughout many lands. According to ancient Syriac Christian tradition, the Apostle Thomas appointed Thaddeus, also known as Addai, to travel to Aram, the land of the Arameans in Syria and Mesopotamia.[1]


Thaddeus journeyed to Urhoy (Edessa), where King Abgar Ukama suffered from a severe illness and sought healing after hearing reports of Jesus Christ.[2]


When Thaddeus arrived in Edessa, he preached openly in the city about the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Many Arameans gathered to hear him speak.[3]


Through the power of God, Thaddeus healed the sick, cast out evil spirits, and performed signs that amazed the people. King Abgar believed in Christ after hearing the message of the apostle.[4]


Thaddeus then prayed over the king, and Abgar was healed from his illness.[5]


The conversion of the king greatly influenced the people of Edessa, and many accepted the Christian faith. The Arameans of Edessa became the first nation to accept Christianity.[6]


Thaddeus baptized new believers and established one of the earliest Christian communities in the region. The Church of Edessa later became an important center of Syriac Christianity, preserving the Aramaic language spoken by Jesus and his disciples.[7]


Thaddeus continued preaching throughout Syria and nearby regions, teaching repentance, faith, and love for God. His mission helped spread Christianity among the Arameans, laying the foundation for the growth of the Syriac Church for centuries to come.[8]


Ancient Christian writings remember him as one of the first apostles to bring the Gospel to the peoples of Mesopotamia and Syria.[9]
